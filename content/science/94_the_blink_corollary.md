## The Common Saying

"Don't blink or you'll miss it."

Meaning: things happen fast, stay alert, keep watching.

## The Corollary

**If you don't blink, you WILL miss it.**

Meaning: the interruption is what lets you see. Without the blink, you go blind.

---

## The Biology

A human blinks **15-20 times per minute**. Each blink lasts 100-400 milliseconds. That's 10% of your waking life spent with your eyes closed.

Why? Not just to lubricate. The blink:

1. **Resets the retina.** Photoreceptors fatigue under continuous stimulation. The brief darkness lets them reset. Without it: retinal adaptation — the signal becomes indistinguishable from background.

2. **Triggers the refresh.** The brain doesn't process vision continuously. It processes in **frames**, bounded by saccades and blinks. Each blink is a frame boundary. Without frame boundaries: no before/after comparison, no delta detection, no change perception.

3. **Prevents habituation.** Stare at anything long enough and it disappears. This is called Troxler's fading — your visual cortex suppresses unchanging stimuli. The blink interrupts the suppression. Without it: the unchanging becomes invisible.

4. **Enables the saccade.** The eye doesn't track smoothly — it jumps (saccades). During each jump, you're functionally blind (~50ms). The brain fills in. The blindness IS part of the seeing. The gap IS part of the signal.

**If you don't blink, you literally cannot see change.**

## The Formal Statement

**The Blink Corollary**: For any observation system with temporal sampling:

*Continuous observation produces habituation, not perception. The interruption between observations is where change is registered. Removing the gap removes the ability to detect delta. The blink rate must be ≥ 2× the frequency of the phenomenon to avoid aliasing (Nyquist). The gap IS the measurement.*

Or more simply:

**If you don't blink, you will miss it.**
